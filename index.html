Chapter 1 · Integration

The Sinking Boat

Alex and Keyshawn cling to their rowboat as waves slap the sides. Through the spray they see the island—but a jagged hole in the hull gurgles below the waterline. Alex remembers the boat's cross-section: from the keel to the gunwale on one side, the depth profile is y = 9 − x² metres, measured from the centreline out to x = 3 m. The boat holds 27 m³ of air before it floods. Water enters through the hole at a constant 0.9 m³/min.

Combined integral problem

Find (A) the cross-sectional area of one side of the hull and (B) how many minutes until the boat is completely submerged.

Part A — Area A = ∫₀³ (9 − x²) dx

Part B — Time to sink t = Volume ÷ inflow rate = 27 ÷ 0.9

Select the pair that matches both calculations.

    Chapter 2 · Related Rates

    Race to the Shore

    The boat slips under. Alex and Keyshawn strike out for the island. The shore is a straight line; distance from shore is measured in metres. At this instant, Alex is 80 m from shore swimming at 1.0 m/s toward land. Keyshawn is 50 m from shore swimming at 1.4 m/s toward land along the same path. Alex is behind Keyshawn, chasing the same point on the beach.

    Related rates

    Let D(t) be the distance between them. How fast is Keyshawn pulling away from Alex at this instant—that is, find dD/dt?

    D = (distance of Alex from shore) − (distance of Keyshawn from shore)
    D = (80 − 1.0t) − (50 − 1.4t) = 30 + 0.4t
    Differentiate with respect to t.

      Island Survival

      They crawl onto the abandoned island, soaked but alive. A crate of supplies holds exactly 150 kg of food. Their combined consumption (in kg) after t full days on the island is modelled by C(t) = 6t², so food remaining is F(t) = 150 − 6t². They starve when F(t) = 0.

      Application of derivatives
